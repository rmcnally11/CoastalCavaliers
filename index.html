One cycle, four moves

How it works

A fixed cutoff is what makes small makers reliable. Your baker can commit to forty loaves with two days’ notice. He cannot bake six on a Saturday whim, and neither can we.

01

Order by Wednesday noon

Build your box from the week’s manifest, or take the captain’s pick. Late orders roll to the next cycle. We do not negotiate the cutoff, because the people baking your bread cannot.

02

We buy it that day

Every maker gets a purchase order Wednesday afternoon for exactly what sold. Nothing is made on spec. Nothing sits on a shelf waiting for you.

03

Packed Friday

Assembled to a pack sheet, cold items last with gel packs, every item labelled with the hand that made it and the port it came from.

04

Aboard before you leave

Friday evening in the marina cooler for a dawn start, or handed over Saturday morning. Sequenced to your departure time, not ours.

Straight answers

Questions we actually get.

What happens if I order after the Wednesday cutoff?

Your order rolls to the next cycle and we tell you so on the confirmation, immediately. We do not squeeze it in. The cutoff is what lets a one‑person bakery commit to your loaf, and bending it for one order breaks it for everyone in that batch.

Where exactly do you deliver?

To your marina, not your boat — a staffed counter, or a cooler we intend to supply and service when the first box goes out. That is deliberate: it keeps the route tight enough to be affordable, and it means nothing sits baking on a hot dock waiting for you. Choose Friday evening staging for a dawn departure, or Saturday morning handover.

What if a maker comes up short?

Every cottage item has a designated licensed backup. If the bread does not arrive, the box is not short — it is different, and we tell you before you get to the dock. Two misses in a season and the backup becomes the primary.

Is home‑kitchen food inspected?

No, and Texas law requires us to say so on the label: made in a home kitchen that is not inspected by the Department of State Health Services. When we sell cottage goods, every item will be labelled with the maker’s name and allergens. Cottage makers we work with will need to be registered with DSHS. We are not a registered vendor today. Anything refrigerated or prepared comes from a licensed commercial kitchen instead — home kitchens are not permitted to make it and we do not ask them to.

How do the makers get paid?

Cottage makers are paid every Friday for the previous cycle, with a one‑page statement. Licensed suppliers are paid net‑15 from receipt, clean counts, no chargebacks. Nobody pays to list, be stocked, or be featured.

Can I add beer or wine?

Not from us. Alcohol in Texas is a separate licensed business under its own three‑tier rules, and we would rather do one thing properly. For yacht and event work we coordinate with a licensed retailer who delivers separately, and we take nothing on it.

I am not in Clear Lake. Now what?

Put your zip in at the top. We open new water where members and makers are already asking for it, and your zip is a vote. When there are enough boats and enough makers within a thirty‑minute loop, that port goes on the calendar — and you will hear first.

Do I have to be a member?

No. Deckhand is free and you pay per box. Membership buys member pricing, peak‑season priority, first call on limited drops and the members‑only gear. If you provision more than five times a season it pays for itself; if you do not, stay free and we will not chase you.

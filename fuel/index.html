For the dockmaster
The idea

One pair of hands between the market and your tank.

Every gallon of boat fuel starts at a terminal, where its price is printed daily on the wholesale market — the same market traders buy and sell all day. By the time that gallon reaches a slip, everyone who touched it has taken a margin, and the last price is set by what the water will bear. Waterdog is one pair of hands: a career gasoline trader buys at the terminal and the truck drives to the water. The savings aren't a discount. They're the layers that aren't there.

The usual way: terminal → distributor → reseller → dock → your tank.

Waterdog: terminal → truck → your tank.

The cycle

Wednesday noon, every week.

Orders close Wednesday at noon. We cut the route, and every slip gets a delivery window. Friday and Saturday the truck runs — every gallon metered, every ticket stamped. Miss the cutoff and you roll to next week, no exceptions, because the cutoff is what keeps the truck full, the route tight, and the price honest.

The covenant

We don’t eat the channel.

We will never wet-hose in a basin where a marina runs a working fuel dock — unless that marina asks us to. A hose on somebody else’s water is how you lose every wholesale conversation on the coast. If you pump, we’d rather supply you than compete with you.
